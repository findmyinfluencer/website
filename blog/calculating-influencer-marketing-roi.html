A Brand's Guide to Calculating Influencer Marketing ROI

FMI Find My Influencer · May 6, 2026 · 6 min read

“We don't really know if it's working” is something we hear from brands more often than you'd expect, usually from teams that are tracking likes and follower growth but never connected the campaign back to an actual business outcome. ROI measurement doesn't need to be complicated, but it does need to be set up before the campaign starts, not after.

Step 1: Pick one goal metric before you launch

Awareness, traffic, leads or direct sales each require a different measurement setup. Trying to measure all four with equal rigour on a single campaign usually means measuring none of them well. Choose the one that matters most for this specific campaign.

Step 2: Give every creator a trackable link or code

A unique UTM-tagged link or a creator-specific promo code is the single highest-leverage thing you can set up. Without it, you're forced to rely on self-reported screenshots and rough estimates instead of actual attributed traffic or sales.

Step 3: Calculate cost-per-outcome, not cost-per-follower

Cost-per-thousand-impressions is useful for awareness campaigns, but for anything closer to the bottom of the funnel, calculate cost-per-click, cost-per-lead or cost-per-sale using the trackable links from step 2. This is the number that lets you compare influencer marketing directly against your other acquisition channels.

Step 4: Separate content performance from creator performance

A well-performing post from a creator with weak conversion, versus a modest-reach post from a creator whose audience converts well, are different signals. Track both view-level engagement and click-through or conversion rate per creator, since the two metrics often diverge and tell you different things about who to rebook.

A simple benchmark to start from

Most brands running influencer campaigns for the first time see acquisition costs land somewhat higher than their best-performing paid channels initially, then improve significantly by the second or third campaign as they learn which creators and formats convert. Budget for that learning curve rather than judging the channel on a single campaign.

"The brands that treat their first influencer campaign as a paid-media test, with the same rigour as a Google Ads test, are the ones who scale it successfully."
