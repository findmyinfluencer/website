How a Regional Food Delivery Brand Used Nano-Influencers to Launch in Tier-2 Cities

FMI Find My Influencer · March 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Launching a food delivery app city by city across Tier-2 India is a different problem than a single national launch. A regional delivery brand we're calling TastyBite for this case study found that out the hard way after its initial national ad campaign generated downloads concentrated almost entirely in cities it wasn't even ready to serve yet.

The problem with a national campaign for a city-by-city rollout

National paid ads reach everyone at once regardless of which cities are actually live, wasting spend on audiences who can't yet use the app, and diluting budget across a footprint far wider than the launch plan needed in any given month.

Switching to hyperlocal nano-influencers

For each new city launch, TastyBite's team worked with 8-12 nano and micro food and lifestyle creators based specifically in that city, rather than running a single influencer campaign across the whole country. Each creator posted about the launch offer, tagged their specific city, and directed followers to check if delivery was live in their area.

  • City-specific creators only, matched to each individual launch date
  • Localised offers and language: creators referenced neighbourhoods and local restaurant partners by name
  • A staggered rollout matching the actual pace of the city-by-city launch plan

Results

Cost-per-download in newly-launched cities came in significantly lower than the earlier national campaign's blended average, and first-week order volume per new city launch improved as well, since the audience reached was, by definition, already in a serviceable location.

Why hyperlocal worked better than national reach here

A nano-influencer based in a specific city, mentioning a specific neighbourhood and specific restaurant partners, reads as local knowledge rather than an ad. For a business whose value proposition is inherently location-bound, matching the marketing footprint to the actual service footprint avoided the wasted reach that came with a broader campaign.
