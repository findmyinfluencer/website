How a Home Decor Brand Used Local Creators to Build Trust Before Launch

FMI Find My Influencer · July 31, 2026 · 6 min read

A home decor brand we're calling Nestcraft Home for this case study faced a common pre-launch problem: a new product line ready to go, no existing audience of its own, and a paid ad budget that wouldn't stretch far enough to build real trust before launch day.

The challenge

Home decor is a considered purchase. Buyers want to see a product styled in a real home before trusting it enough to order, something static product photography and paid ads struggle to convey convincingly on their own.

The approach

Instead of a traditional ad campaign, Nestcraft Home partnered with 12 home and lifestyle creators across three cities two weeks before launch, sending early product samples and asking for genuine home-styling content rather than product-focused promotion.

  • 12 creators across Chennai, Bengaluru and Coimbatore, all in the home or lifestyle niche
  • Each creator styled the product naturally within their own home content, no studio setup
  • Content rolled out over two weeks leading up to launch, not all at once

Results

By launch day, the product had already appeared organically in the feeds of creators with genuinely engaged, relevant audiences, generating pre-orders before any paid advertising began. The staggered rollout also meant the brand had a library of real, in-home content to reuse in its own launch-day posts.

"Seeing the product in someone's actual living room did more for buyer confidence in two weeks than our product photography did in two months."

Why this worked for a considered-purchase category

For products where buyers need to picture the item in their own space before trusting it, creator content showing a genuine home setting consistently outperforms studio photography and paid ads alone. The staggered, pre-launch rollout also meant the brand entered launch day with existing social proof rather than starting from zero.
