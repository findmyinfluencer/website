How to Spot Fake Followers and Engagement Before You Book a Creator

FMI Find My Influencer · July 29, 2026 · 6 min read

A large follower count feels like proof of reach, but it's one of the easiest numbers to inflate. Before committing budget to a creator, a few quick checks can tell you whether that audience is genuinely engaged or mostly bought.

Why follower count alone tells you almost nothing

Followers can be purchased in bulk for a small amount of money, and many accounts carry a mix of real followers and inactive or fake ones without the creator even realising the full extent of it. Follower count should be treated as a starting filter, never the deciding factor.

Check the engagement rate, then check it again

A healthy engagement rate (likes plus comments, divided by followers) typically falls somewhere between 2% and 8% depending on the niche and follower tier. An account with hundreds of thousands of followers but an engagement rate under 1% is a clear warning sign.

Look at the comments, not just the count

Generic comments like single emojis or unrelated phrases repeated across many posts are a strong indicator of bot activity or engagement pods. Genuine engagement usually includes specific reactions to the actual content, tagged friends, and real questions.

Sudden follower spikes are a red flag

A steady, gradual growth curve is normal. A sudden jump of tens of thousands of followers in a short window, with no matching spike in engagement, usually points to a purchased batch rather than organic growth.

Ask for platform-native analytics screenshots

Genuine creators can usually share screenshots directly from their Instagram or YouTube analytics dashboard showing audience demographics and reach. Hesitation or an inability to provide this is worth noting.

Verified creators are the safest starting point
