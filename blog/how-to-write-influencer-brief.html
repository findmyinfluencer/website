How to Write an Influencer Brief Creators Actually Follow

FMI Find My Influencer · June 10, 2026 · 5 min read

Most underperforming influencer content isn't the creator's fault, it's the brief's. Either it's so vague the creator has to guess what you actually want, or so over-specified that the final post reads like a script read aloud rather than a real recommendation. Good briefs sit in a narrow, specific middle ground.

Lead with the single most important message

If a creator's audience only remembers one thing from the post, what should it be? Every brief should open by naming that one message clearly, before any of the logistical details. If you can't articulate it in one sentence, the campaign goal probably isn't clear enough yet either.

Give context, not a script

Explain why the product matters, who it's for, and what problem it solves, then let the creator translate that into their own words and format. A script tells the creator what to say; context lets them say it in a way their specific audience will actually respond to.

What to specify exactly

  • Deadline for content submission and go-live date
  • Required hashtags, brand tags and any disclosure requirements
  • Format: Reel, Story, static post, or a combination
  • Any claims that legally can't be made, or must be worded a specific way
  • Whether the brand wants to review a draft before it goes live

What to leave open

Camera angle, tone of voice, specific phrasing, whether they film indoors or outdoors, background music choice. These decisions are exactly where a creator's instinct for their own audience adds value that a brand marketer sitting outside that community usually can't replicate.

Review drafts for message accuracy, not style

If you've asked to review content before it goes live, resist the urge to rewrite it into your own voice. Check that the key message and required elements are present and accurate, and leave the delivery to the person whose audience it actually is.
