Influencer Contracts 101: Clauses Every Brand Should Include

FMI Find My Influencer · August 10, 2026 · 6 min read

A lot of first campaigns run on a DM agreement and a payment. That works fine until a post goes up late, gets deleted early, or a competitor deal shows up the following week. A short written contract prevents most of these disputes before they start.

Deliverables, in writing

Spell out the exact number of posts, stories or videos, the platform for each, and the minimum content requirements, rather than a vague "a few posts about the product."

Usage rights

State exactly how long the brand can reuse the content, and on which channels, whitelisted ads, the brand's own social pages, a website. Without this clause, reusing a creator's content beyond the original post can become a dispute after the fact.

Exclusivity windows

If the brand wants the creator to avoid posting for a direct competitor, define the exact window and category. Open-ended exclusivity clauses are a common source of creator pushback and are rarely necessary.

Approval and revision terms

Agree on how many rounds of revision are included and the turnaround time for brand approval, so a delay on either side doesn't blow past the campaign's posting window.

Payment terms and timing

Fix the payment split, for example half upfront and half on posting, and the exact number of days after content goes live that final payment is due.

What happens if a post is deleted early

Set a minimum live duration and the consequence if content is taken down before it, whether that's a partial refund or a make-good post.
