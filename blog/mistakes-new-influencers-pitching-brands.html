5 Mistakes New Influencers Make When Pitching Brands

FMI Find My Influencer · June 3, 2026 · 5 min read

Cold-pitching brands directly is one of the fastest ways for a new creator to land a first paid collaboration, but most pitches fail before they're even fully read. The good news is that the reasons are predictable, and every one of them is fixable in an afternoon.

1. Sending the same generic message to dozens of brands

Brand marketers can spot a copy-pasted pitch instantly, usually because it doesn't mention the product by name or says nothing that couldn't apply to any brand in the category. A pitch that references a specific recent product or campaign takes five extra minutes to write and dramatically improves response rates.

2. No media kit or stats attached

Asking a brand to click through to your profile to find your own numbers is asking them to do work they usually won't bother with. Attach your stats, or at minimum your engagement rate and audience location, directly in the first message.

3. Pitching brands outside your actual niche

A fitness creator pitching a fintech app, or vice versa, rarely lands regardless of how good the pitch itself is. Brands are filtering for niche fit first. Before pitching, ask honestly whether your existing content would make this specific product feel natural to your audience.

4. An unclear ask

“Let me know if you'd like to collaborate” puts all the work of figuring out logistics back on the brand. A stronger pitch proposes something concrete: one Reel and a Story, in exchange for product plus a specific rate, with a rough timeline.

5. No follow-up

Most brand inboxes are genuinely busy, and a good pitch can get buried, not rejected. A single polite follow-up after a week converts more often than people expect, as long as it's brief and doesn't come across as pressure.

"The creators who get booked repeatedly aren't necessarily the most talented, they're the ones who make it easiest for a brand to say yes quickly."
