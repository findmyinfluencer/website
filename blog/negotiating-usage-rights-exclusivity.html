A Creator's Guide to Negotiating Usage Rights and Exclusivity Clauses

FMI Find My Influencer · April 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Two clauses in a brand collaboration agreement do more to determine its real value than the headline rate: usage rights and exclusivity. Both are easy to skim past and easy to underprice, especially for creators negotiating their first few deals.

What usage rights actually mean

Usage rights determine what a brand can do with your content beyond the organic post on your own account. A brand asking to run your Reel as a paid ad, repost it on their own channels, or use it in a TV or print campaign is asking for something meaningfully bigger than a one-time organic post, and it should be priced separately.

Questions to ask before agreeing to usage rights

  • Where exactly will the content be used: the brand's own channels, paid ads, or both?
  • For how long: 30 days, 6 months, in perpetuity?
  • Exclusively for this brand, or could they license it to a third party?

What exclusivity clauses mean

An exclusivity clause restricts you from working with competing brands for a defined period. This is a real cost to you, since it closes off other opportunities in that category while it's active, and should always come with additional compensation beyond the base rate for the deliverable itself.

Reasonable terms to aim for

Usage rights are commonly priced as an additional percentage on top of the base creative fee, often scaling with duration and where the content will run. Exclusivity is typically time-bound and category-specific (for example, “no other skincare brands for 60 days”) rather than an open-ended, all-category restriction, which is worth pushing back on if offered.

Get it in writing, every time

Verbal or DM-based agreements on these terms are difficult to enforce later. A short written confirmation covering deliverable, usage rights scope and duration, exclusivity terms if any, and payment terms protects both sides and takes minutes to put together.

"The rate is often the easiest part of a negotiation. Usage rights and exclusivity are where creators leave the most money on the table by not asking."
