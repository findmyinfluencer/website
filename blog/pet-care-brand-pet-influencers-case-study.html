How a Pet Care Brand Used Pet Influencers to Triple Sign-Ups

FMI Find My Influencer · August 23, 2026 · 6 min read

A pet food subscription startup had tried general lifestyle influencers for two campaigns with underwhelming sign-up numbers. Their third attempt shifted entirely to accounts run around a specific pet, and the results looked nothing like the first two.

The problem with lifestyle creators for this category

A pet appearing occasionally in a lifestyle creator's feed doesn't carry the same trust as an account an audience follows specifically for pet content, where product recommendations feel like advice rather than an ad.

Switching to dedicated pet accounts

The brand booked 18 creators running accounts built entirely around their pets, dogs and cats across a mix of breed sizes, to better match the subscription's product variants.

Content built around a real trial, not a mention

Each creator documented a two-week trial of the subscription with their own pet, rather than a single sponsored post, giving followers a believable before-and-after rather than a one-off endorsement.

Tracking with dedicated sign-up links

Unique referral links per creator let the brand see exactly which accounts converted, rather than judging the campaign on likes and comments alone.

The results

Sign-ups roughly tripled compared to the brand's earlier lifestyle-creator campaigns, with pet-focused accounts converting at a significantly higher rate despite smaller average follower counts.
