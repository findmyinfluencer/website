How to Price Your First Brand Collaboration

FMI Find My Influencer · July 15, 2026 · 5 min read

“What should I charge?” is the question every new creator asks first, and the one with the least useful answers online. Generic follower-based rate cards ignore the things that actually decide what a brand should pay you: engagement, niche and what exactly they're asking for.

Start with engagement rate, not follower count

Two creators with 50K followers can be worth very different amounts. One with a 6% engagement rate is delivering far more attention per follower than one at 1.5%. Calculate your own rate (average likes plus comments, divided by followers, over your last 10 posts) before you quote anyone a number.

Price the deliverable, not the collaboration

“A collaboration” isn't a price point. A single Instagram Story, a Reel, a static feed post and a YouTube integration all take different amounts of effort and reach different audiences. Break every brief into individual deliverables and price each one separately.

  • Instagram Story (1-3 frames): quickest to produce, shortest lifespan
  • Feed post or Reel: highest production effort, longest visibility
  • YouTube integration or dedicated video: most time-intensive, highest rate

Factor in usage rights and exclusivity

If a brand wants to reuse your content in their own ads, or wants exclusivity in your niche for a period, that's worth more than a one-time organic post. Usage rights and exclusivity clauses should always carry a separate line item in your quote, not be bundled in for free.

Niche matters more than category averages

A finance or tech creator with a smaller, highly-engaged audience can often charge more than a lifestyle creator with double the followers, because the audience is harder to reach and more likely to convert. Know where your niche sits before comparing your rates to a generic benchmark.

Don't undercharge to “get your foot in the door”

Working for free or heavily discounted rates to build a portfolio rarely pays off the way creators hope. It trains brands to expect free work, and it's difficult to raise your rate with a brand you've already worked with cheaply. A better foot-in-the-door is a smaller, clearly-scoped deliverable at your real rate.

Put a simple rate card together

You don't need anything fancy: a one-page breakdown of your rate per deliverable type, your average engagement rate, and your audience demographics is enough to start every negotiation from a position of clarity rather than guessing on the spot.
