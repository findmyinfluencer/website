Why Tamil and Regional-Language Creators Are Outperforming English-Only Accounts

FMI Find My Influencer · July 31, 2026 · 6 min read

English-language content still gets a lot of attention in influencer marketing conversations, but some of the strongest engagement rates we track on the platform come from creators posting primarily in Tamil, Hindi and other regional languages. Here's what's driving that, and what it means for both sides.

The audience English-only content misses

A large share of India's internet audience is far more comfortable consuming, and trusting, content in their own first language. English-only content, even when well produced, simply doesn't reach or resonate with that audience the same way.

Regional language builds deeper trust

Content in a viewer's own language tends to feel more like a recommendation from someone in their own community, rather than a broadcast. That difference shows up directly in comment quality, saves and, ultimately, conversion.

What this means for brands

Brands targeting Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities, or specific regional markets within a state like Tamil Nadu, often see better results from regional-language creators than from a larger, English-only account with a diluted national audience.

What this means for creators

Creators posting in Tamil or another regional language shouldn't assume a smaller-looking follower count means less value to a brand. Engagement quality and audience trust often more than make up for a lower raw reach number.

You don't have to choose just one language

Many of the best-performing creators we see mix languages within a single piece of content, speaking primarily in Tamil with English product names or captions, which keeps content accessible while staying authentic to how people actually talk.

Filtering creators by language is built into the platform. Search by language and city to find the right regional fit for your next campaign.
