UGC vs Influencer Marketing: What's the Difference and Which Do You Need?

FMI Find My Influencer · July 29, 2026 · 5 min read

User-generated content (UGC) and influencer marketing often get lumped together because both involve creators making content about your product. In practice they solve different problems, and knowing the difference changes who you should be booking and why.

What UGC actually means

UGC is content created to look and feel authentic, typically produced by a creator specifically for the brand to reuse in its own ads, website or social channels. The creator's own following and reach usually matter far less than the quality and believability of the content itself.

What influencer marketing actually means

Influencer marketing centres on the creator's own audience. The content is posted on the creator's account, to their followers, and its value comes largely from their existing trust and reach within that community.

The real difference: reach vs raw authenticity

If your goal is authentic-feeling creative you can run as paid ads to a cold audience, UGC is usually the more cost-effective route, since you're paying for content, not reach. If your goal is to actually reach a creator's existing, trusting audience, you need influencer marketing, not UGC.

When UGC alone is enough

Early-stage testing of ad creative, product demo videos, and testimonial-style content for a website or landing page are all situations where UGC alone can work well, often at a lower cost than a full influencer campaign.

When you need an actual creator's audience

Launch campaigns, awareness building in a new city or niche, and anything relying on social proof from a specific community all need the creator's real reach, not just content that looks like it came from a creator.

Most brands eventually need both

A common, effective pattern is running an influencer campaign first for reach and credibility, then repurposing the best-performing organic content as paid ad creative, essentially getting both formats from a single well-briefed campaign.
